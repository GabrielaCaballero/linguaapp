# 01 · Curriculum: A0 → B1 for Italian and German

_Curriculum curator (teacher), 2026-11-12. Read with `00-brief.md`. No code changed._

**TL;DR**: Follow the order that the big textbooks agree on: sounds and greetings → "who am I" with *to be*, *to be called* and *to come from* → numbers → articles and gender with food and shopping → family → present tense and daily routine → the first past tense at the end of A1. Store each item once in the target language, with glosses in **both es and en**, and put the Spanish↔Italian "bridge" material in an optional layer. v2 ships 6 units per language, about 120 items each.

---

## 1. Sequencing principles

| # | Principle | What it means for Lingua | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Communicative need first, grammar in service of it** | Every unit starts with a can-do goal ("introduce yourself"). Grammar shows up when the goal needs it, so *ich bin / ich heiße* comes before the full conjugation table. | CEFR can-do descriptors (Council of Europe 2001/2020); Profile Deutsch; *Profilo della lingua italiana* (Spinelli & Parizzi 2010) |
| 2 | **Frequency** | Early vocabulary comes from the official level word lists. Goethe A1 has about 650 entries, and roughly half should be active. A2 has about 1,300 lexical units. | Goethe-Zertifikat A1/A2 Wortlisten |
| 3 | **Grammar spiral** | Each structure appears three times: first as an unanalysed chunk (*vorrei*, *ich möchte*), then as a pattern, then as a full paradigm. The past tense first appears as a chunk in U5–6 and is taught properly at the end of A1. | Standard in Menschen, Netzwerk neu, Nuovo Espresso and Progetto italiano |
| 4 | **Comprehensible input** | Each unit has listening and dialogue items that are about 95% known plus a few new items. | Krashen; Nation (98% coverage for unassisted reading) |
| 5 | **Four strands balance** | Each unit gives roughly equal weight to meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning and fluency (speed round). | Nation, *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language* |
| 6 | **Pronunciation early, in small doses** | Unit 1 covers the sound-spelling rules that cause most errors (it: c/g, gli, gn, sc, doubles; de: umlauts, ch, ei/ie, sch/sp/st, final devoicing). Later units revisit one sound each. | Every textbook surveyed opens with sounds and the alphabet |
| 7 | **Use L1 transfer, flag interference** | Spanish→Italian is about 80% lexically similar (the commonly cited Ethnologue figure; the linguist should verify it). Teach the cognate rules explicitly, and teach false friends as soon as they come up. | Contrastive analysis; v1 already had this in M0 |

## 2. Unit maps A0 → B1

### Study-hour estimates (cumulative, from zero)

| Level | Cambridge guided hours (class + guided homework) | Goethe course reality | Words (receptive) | **Lingua target, en speaker** | **Lingua target, es speaker → Italian** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 90–100 h | 2×75 units of 45 min ≈ 110 h; Nuovo Espresso 1 ≈ 90 h of material | ~650 | ~90 h | ~60 h |
| A2 | 180–200 h | +~110 h | ~1,300 | ~190 h | ~130 h |
| B1 | 350–400 h | +~120 h; B1 courses run 80 units of 45 min each | ~2,400 (to verify against the B1 list) | ~375 h | ~250 h |

- FSI (time to professional level for English speakers): Italian takes 24–30 weeks, or 600–750 class hours. German takes about 36 weeks, or 900 hours. So **German needs about 20–30% more time than Italian for English speakers.** For German, use the top of each band.
- **Spanish speakers learning Italian:** shared vocabulary, morphology (gender, -o/-a, clitics, subjunctive) and phonology cut the time needed. I suggest a **0.65–0.7 multiplier** on hours. This is the teacher's estimate, not a published figure, and the learning scientist needs to validate it. Spanish speakers learning German get no advantage over English speakers, and possibly a slight disadvantage, because there are fewer cognates.
- These are hours of guided study. App minutes count fully only for practice that is active and spaced. The PM goal calculator should convert "level by date" into minutes per day using these figures.

**Spanish-base adjustments for Italian**: move faster through vocabulary. Spend time instead on the places where Spanish speakers make mistakes: article forms (*lo/gli/l'*), double consonants, possessive + article, choosing *essere* or *avere* in the passato prossimo, *ci/ne*, prepositions with articles, and false friends. The grammar is very similar (subjunctive and clitics both transfer), so the B1 subjunctive will come more easily for them than for English speakers.

## 3. First slice for v2 (A0 → A1.1, units 1–6 per language)

### Item types and the exercises each supports

| Item type | What it is | Exercises |
|---|---|---|
| `sound` | grapheme ↔ sound rule plus 1–3 example words | listen → pick spelling; minimal pairs (*nono/nonno*, *Stadt/Staat*); read aloud with speech check |
| `word` | lemma with gender, plural and forms | flashcard both directions; MCQ; typing; listen → type; picture/emoji match; article drill (de/it) |
| `phrase` | fixed chunk (*Wie geht's?*, *Piacere*) | situational MCQ ("you meet your boss…"); type; say it aloud |
| `sentence` | model sentence with slots | translate; build from word tiles; cloze; transform (du→Sie, tu→Lei) |
| `pattern` | grammar pattern with paradigm and rule | guess-first discovery; fill the form; pick the article; correct the error; sort (der/die/das) |
| `listening` | short audio text (TTS) + question | listen → answer MCQ; dictation; true/false |
| `dialogue` | 4–8 turns with roles | read/listen; choose the next line; play role A or B (free answer, LLM-gradable) |
| `bridge` (optional layer) | cognate rule or false friend for one base language | guess-first pattern ("-ción → ?"); "this means…" trap MCQ |

Target mix per unit: about 50% words, 20% phrases and sentences, 10% patterns, 5–10% sounds, 10% listening and dialogue.

**Bridge layer for Italian from Spanish** (available in units 1–6 when the base language is es): the v1 M0 patterns (-ción→-zione, -dad→-tà, ie→e, ue→o, ll→chi, j→gli) and false friends (*burro, caldo, salire, camera, gamba, imbarazzata, largo, subito, topo, cugino/primo*). Attach each one to the unit where the word first appears; don't put them all in a separate module. Example: *caldo* goes in U4 with the bar, *camera* in U6.
**Bridge layer for German from English**: cognates (Haus/house, Wasser/water, Milch/milk, -ung) and false friends (*Gift*, *bekommen*, *also*, *Chef*, *Handy*). German from Spanish gets a small set: *Gymnasium*, *Rente*, *Mantel*, *Tablette* and similar.

**Remove from v1 content:** all Lavello, comune, atto di nascita and Zurigo items, and the "Gabriela" name, as the brief requires. Keep the sound, pattern and false-friend items. They become U1 sounds plus the bridge layer.

## 4. Content data model (proposal)

```ts
type Base = "es" | "en";               // add more later
type Target = "it" | "de";
type ItemType = "sound" | "word" | "phrase" | "sentence" | "pattern" | "listening" | "dialogue" | "bridge";

interface ContentItem {
  id: string;               // "de-u04-mutter" — stable, never reused
  lang: Target;
  unit: string;             // "de-u04"
  type: ItemType;
  cefr: "A0" | "A1.1" | "A1.2" | "A2" | "B1";
  target: string;           // canonical form: "die Mutter", "la madre"
  accept?: string[];        // alternatives that count as correct
  variants?: { region: "DE"|"AT"|"CH"|"IT"; text: string }[]; // Grüezi, Swiss "ss"
  gloss: Record<Base, string>;            // { es: "la madre", en: "the mother" }
  grammar?: {
    pos: "noun"|"verb"|"adj"|"adv"|"prep"|"pron"|"num"|"phrase"|"other";
    gender?: "m"|"f"|"n"; plural?: string;            // "die Mütter"
    forms?: Record<string, string>;                   // { ich:"bin", du:"bist", "3sg":"ist" } / participle, aux
  };
  example?: { target: string; gloss: Record<Base, string> };
  audio: { text: string; slow?: boolean };          // TTS text; may differ from target (e.g. "zweiundzwanzig" for "22")
  notes?: Partial<Record<Base, string>>;            // teaching note written for that base language
  bridge?: Partial<Record<Base, {                   // OPTIONAL layer
    kind: "cognate-pattern" | "false-friend" | "transfer-tip";
    text: string; distractors?: string[];
  }>>;
  pattern?: { rule: Record<Base,string>; table?: string[][] };  // for type "pattern"
  turns?: { who: "A"|"B"; target: string; gloss: Record<Base,string> }[]; // dialogue
  question?: { prompt: Record<Base,string>; choices: string[]; answer: string }; // listening
  freq?: number;            // rank in a frequency list where available
  tags: string[];           // "food", "polite", "goethe-a1", "chunk"
  reviewed?: { by: string; date: string };          // linguist sign-off
}
```

- **Both es and en glosses on every item from v2.** It costs little, the linguist can review both, and it means base language really is just a setting (orchestrator decision 2).
- `notes` are **written separately per base language**, not translated. A Spanish speaker needs "gli is like a lateral ll". An English speaker needs "like the lli in million".
- **Keep the bridge layer and make it optional.** For es→it it is the biggest accelerator Spanish speakers have. It must be tied to a base language, because English speakers get nothing from "-dad → -tà". It is shown when it applies and can be hidden in Settings.
- German: **standard German (DE) spelling with ß is the canonical form.** CH (ss) and AT forms go in `variants` and are always accepted. Swiss-only words are tagged `region:CH` and are optional.

## 5. Placement test (content side)

- **Step 0, self-report:** "Never studied / some words / can hold a simple conversation / more". "Never" skips the test and starts at U1, so absolute beginners don't have to fail 20 questions.
- **Bands and sampling:** 4 bands (A1.1, A1.2, A2, B1) × 6 items = **24 items maximum, about 5–7 minutes.** Each band has 2 vocabulary items (frequency-sampled from that band's list, recognition MCQ), 2 grammar items (key structures: verb second / articles at A1.1, Perfekt or passato prossimo at A1.2, dative / *weil* or imperfetto / pronouns at A2, Konjunktiv II / passive or congiuntivo at B1), 1 listening item (TTS) and 1 short reading item.
- **Adaptive stop:** start at the band the self-report suggests. Score ≥ 5/6 → move up. Score ≤ 3/6 → stop and test the band below if it hasn't been tested yet.
- **Mapping:** the starting unit is the **first unit of the highest band passed + 1**. Pass = 5/6; 4/6 = start at the middle of that band. Items in passed bands go into SRS as "probably known" with long first intervals. If a learner misses them, they drop back to normal intervals. Placement mistakes then fix themselves in the first week.
- **Item pool:** 3 parallel items per slot (about 72 per language), so a retake is not the same test.
- **Spanish speakers learning Italian:** cognate vocabulary inflates recognition scores. Count grammar, false-friend and listening items more heavily for es→it, and include at least one false friend per band.
- Include an "I don't know" option on every item to cut guessing (the learning scientist should confirm the scoring).
